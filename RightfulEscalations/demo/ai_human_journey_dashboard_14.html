Coaching Playbook — Human Agent Recovery
Ranked by recovery impact · filtered to post-handover behaviors
🔴 Immediate · Priority 1
Own the Context — Don't Ask What the Bot Already Captured
Only 61% of transfers include full AI context. Agents who re-ask questions already answered by the AI score 22pts lower on Journey QM and trigger a -0.8 sentiment drop. Coach agents to use the AI summary panel before speaking.
+22pts QM +0.8 sentiment 312 affected contacts
🔴 Immediate · Priority 2
Acknowledge the Wait — Every Time
Agents who acknowledge the transfer wait within 10 seconds achieve 88% recovery vs 62% for those who don't. Script: "Thank you for holding — I can see you were just with our automated assistant. Let me pick this up from where it left off."
+26pts recovery 0:48 avg wait to address
🟡 Near-Term · Priority 3
Special-Handling Exception Authority
18% of rightful escalations involve special-handling (medical equipment, travel exceptions). Agents stall on who can authorize. Push the exception decision tree into Agent Assist. Reduces handle time by 1m 10s per contact.
-1:10 handle time Agent Assist
🟡 Near-Term · Priority 4
Cancellation Recovery Script
Cancellation intent has the lowest recovery rate (61%) and the only negative sentiment trend (-0.3). FAQ Bot is escalating 58% of these unnecessarily. Coach agents on retention language and flag the bot's FAQ gap for prompt update.
+14pts potential 198 contacts/wk
🔵 Coaching · Priority 5
Close the Loop — Confirm Resolution Before Wrap
Only 74% of agents explicitly confirm resolution in transcript. Gap between transcript confirmation (74%) and 7-day held resolution (82%) suggests under-reporting, not actual failure. Coach agents to ask and document "Was your issue fully resolved today?"

🔵 Coaching · Priority 6
Top Recoverers — Replicate the Playbook
3 agents recover 88% vs the team's 62% on special-handling contacts. Same contacts, same intents — the gap is human handling. Surface their calls in team calibration and extract the behavioral pattern for peer coaching.
